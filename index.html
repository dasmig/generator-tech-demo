    ⭐ Persona Generator

    Generates a complete fictional persona with persona-generator, which composes eight libraries through entity-generator fields: country, city, birthday, body, name, personality, religion, hobbies, favourite genres and occupation in one call. Each field is conditioned on the ones it depends on (the country sets the city, the name culture, the body and the job; the age and sex set the rest), and the persona's seed replays all of it. This is fictional sample data, not a description or prediction of a real person. GitHub →

    One person, everywhere

    Runs the persona, address, phone, email, device, vehicle and weather libraries for one country with one call seed. The persona sets the country, name, age and city; the rest follow from it (the email is built from the persona's name and birth year, the weather is the persona's city today). It is the demos below composed in the page, not a pipeline: the vehicle-generator and weather-generator are not part of persona-generator yet. A part the country has no data for is marked, not hidden. Fictional sample data only.

    trait-generator

    Generate fictional personality traits and resolved Big Five scores. Scores are centered on zero before trait effects, not percentages or a personality assessment. GitHub →

    Use 1–16 digits (0–9, a–f), without 0x. Replay with the output seed and the same trait count.

    Each score starts with a normal draw (mean 0, standard deviation 1) and adds the selected traits' catalogue loadings. Base values also influence trait selection; exclusive groups, conflicts and synergies constrain the draw. These are unbounded model values, not measured traits or percentiles.

    occupation-generator

    Generate a fictional employment status, education level and job from country, sex and age inputs, using ILO labour force statistics. Compare the four education levels while holding the country, sex and seed fixed. Outputs are synthetic, not predictions about individuals or groups. GitHub →

    weather-generator

    A year of daily weather for any point on land, drawn around the CRU TS 1991-2020 normals of the nearest cell: wet and dry spells, warm and cold runs, rain, snow, thunder, cloud, wind and the sun. Click the map to choose a place, then pick a day on the chart for its 24 hours. GitHub →

    Each year is one seeded series: a day depends only on the seed, the place and the date, so the day's hours below are that day of the year above. The dashed line is the cell's normal mean temperature. Points more than 100 km from land have no weather. The library can also draw 1991-2020 as it was, from a 23 MB table this page does not load.

    address-generator

    Generate fictional postal addresses with real street names (from OpenStreetMap, weighted by how many addresses each street has), in each country's own layout and postcode format. The street data is a data pack: the page loads the shared tables at start and a country's streets only when you ask for that country, so nothing you do not use is downloaded. Outputs are synthetic. GitHub →

    “Any” picks a country uniformly in this demo, so its streets can be fetched first; the library itself draws countries by population. Compare scripts draws one call seed and shows the address in the country's own layout and in the Latin one; only 14 countries have a Latin layout, and a street keeps its local name when the data has no Latin one.

    vehicle-generator

    Generate a car or motorcycle someone owns in a country at a date, with parts that agree: a make sold there and a model built in its model year, a fuel as common as it was among that year's new vehicles, a trim, gearbox and drive (a motorcycle's final drive is a chain, belt, shaft or hub) the model was offered with, a colour of its era, an odometer that fits its age, a licence plate in the format the country issued on the registration date, and a VIN with the maker's prefix, check digit and model-year code. The safe option gives plates no authority issues and VINs whose maker prefix NHTSA has no record of. Outputs are synthetic; nothing is looked up in a register. GitHub →

    “Any” country is drawn by the vehicles in use of the chosen kind, and “Any” kind by the country's mix of cars and motorcycles. A body belongs to a kind, so picking a kind limits the body list. The plate regions are the country's: US states, Canadian provinces and Australian states pick that jurisdiction's plates, and German districts, Swiss cantons or Japanese offices the code the plate carries; without a country, one that has the region is drawn. A motorcycle has no doors, and petrol mopeds of 50 cc and under are not drawn. The date (today by default, 2000 to 2050) sets the fleet's age, the fuels and the plate format of each registration. Compare countries draws one call seed in Germany, the United Kingdom, Spain, the United States and Japan.

    phone-generator

    Generate fictional phone numbers that are valid in a country's numbering plan, drawn from libphonenumber's mobile and fixed-line ranges and written the way the country writes them. The safe option uses the numbers regulators reserve for fiction (for example 555-01xx in the US and Canada) where a country has such a range; elsewhere it avoids the allocated ranges. Outputs are synthetic. GitHub →

    “Any” country is drawn by population. Compare safe draws one call seed and shows the number with and without the safe option; the type may differ when the country has a reserved range for only one type. “Reserved for fiction” is Yes only for numbers from a regulator's range.

    email-generator

    Generate fictional email addresses in a country's style from a name, nickname and birth year, with the providers people use there. Leave the names empty for a handle.     device-generator

    Generate the device someone uses to reach a service, with parts that agree: a model on sale at the date, an OS version it runs, a browser that runs on that OS at a version released by then, the user agent and client hints that exact combination sends, its screen, and an IP address registered in the country. The safe option gives documentation addresses only (RFC 5737, RFC 3849). Outputs are synthetic. GitHub →

    “Any” country is drawn by internet users. The date defaults to today and picks the models, OS and browser versions out by then; a date after the data's newest release is seen as that release's date. The locale only sets the Accept-Language header. Compare dates sees one call seed in June 2021 to 2024 and today: the country, platform and address stay, the model and versions move. A registered address belongs to the country's blocks and may be in use.

    user-generator

    Generate fictional user accounts for apps and test data: a person with a username, email, phone number and postal address that agree on the country, plus a password, sign-up and last-login times, a platform, a locale and a time zone, and, if asked, the device of the last sign-in (from device-generator) with its user agent and IP address. The safe option uses reserved example domains, where a country has one a phone number from the range set aside for fiction, and a documentation IP address for the device. Outputs are synthetic; the passwords are test data, never real credentials. GitHub →

    The 100 countries listed are the ones every part has data for. “Any” picks one uniformly in this demo, so its streets can be fetched first; the library itself draws by population. Ages are as of today. The device is the one of the last login (the sign-up for an account that never logged in), on the account's platform and locale; asking for it changes nothing else. Compare safe draws one call seed and shows the same person with and without the safe option.

    Relationships & composition

    Families and friends, and the engine that composes everything else.

    relationship-generator

    Generate the people around a person: parents and grandparents, siblings and half-siblings (twins included), partners, children and grandchildren, with ages, deaths and surnames that follow the country's naming convention. The numbers come from UN fertility, marriage and household statistics. GitHub →

    The person is the one the inputs describe; what is left out is drawn. Ages are as of 1 July 2023, the date of the UN estimates. The same seed and inputs give the same family. Relatives who have died are listed with the age they died at. Stepparents (a parent's new partner, the children they had with the parent and their own children, the stepsiblings) and the household (who the person lives with) are off unless chosen.
